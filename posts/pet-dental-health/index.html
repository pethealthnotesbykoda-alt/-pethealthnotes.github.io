Pet Dental Health: The Guide Most Owners Skip (And Shouldn't)

As a 10-year pet parent, I’ve seen firsthand the devastating effects of dental disease on our beloved companions. The pain, the suffering, and the long-term health consequences can be heartbreaking. But it’s not just about the emotional toll – dental disease is a serious health issue that affects up to 80% of dogs and 70% of cats by the age of three. It’s a staggering statistic, and one that highlights the importance of taking proactive steps to protect our pets’ oral health.

If you notice any of the following symptoms, seek immediate veterinary attention: facial swelling below the eye, a broken tooth with exposed pulp, bleeding from the mouth, or a tooth that appears to have shifted position. These are not watch-and-wait situations, and prompt action can make all the difference in preventing long-term damage and ensuring your pet’s overall health and wellbeing.
