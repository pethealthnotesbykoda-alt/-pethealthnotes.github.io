# Senior Dog Hind Leg Weakness: At-Home Exercise Checklist

> **For senior dogs showing mild to moderate hind leg weakness, wobbling, or stiffness.**  
> *Not a substitute for veterinary care. If your dog is suddenly unable to stand, in severe pain, or has lost bladder/bowel control, contact your vet or ER immediately.*

---

## ✅ Before You Start
Before beginning any exercise plan:
- [ ] Confirm with your vet that exercise is safe for your dog’s condition.
- [ ] Choose a non-slip area (rug, mat, grass, not slippery tile).
- [ ] Have treats, water, and a calm space ready.
- [ ] Keep sessions short (6–10 minutes) and gentle.

---

## 🛑 Section 1: Safety & Red Flags
Stop immediately and call your vet if your dog:
- [ ] Cannot stand or walk on back legs
- [ ] Cries out, yelps, or is vicious when touched
- [ ] Collapses or legs give out completely
- [ ] Loses bladder or bowel control
- [ ] Has sudden severe back/neck pain, especially after jumping or falling

*If you notice these, reduce intensity or pause exercises and contact your vet.*

---

## 📏 Section 2: Exercise Rules (Read Before Each Session)
Before each session, remember:
- **Frequency:** 2–3 short sessions per day (6–10 minutes each).
- **Intensity:** Mild effort, not exhaustion.

**Your dog should be able to:**
- [ ] Breathe comfortably
- [ ] Move without limping or crying
- [ ] Act normally the next day

**Stop if you see:**
- [ ] Whining, yelping, or growling
- [ ] Heavy panting, trembling, or very tense body
- [ ] Refusing to move or collapsing during the session
- [ ] Increased stiffness or lameness the next day

---

## 🐕 Section 3: Core Exercises
*Do these in a safe, non-slip area.*

### Exercise 1: Assisted Sit-to-Stand
**Purpose:** Strengthens hips, hind legs, and core.
- Place dog in a sitting position. Stand behind or beside them comfortably.
- Gently support chest and hindquarters (hands or rear-support harness).
- Encourage them to stand with a calm voice and a treat. Give light support as they rise.
- Praise them once standing, let them rest a few seconds. Gently guide back to sitting.
**Reps:** Week 1 (3-5 reps), Week 2-3 (5-8 reps), Week 4+ (8-10 reps)

### Exercise 2: Controlled Leash Walks
**Purpose:** Maintains muscle, improves circulation, builds confidence.
- Use a harness (not just a collar). Keep leash short and controlled.
- Walk slowly on grass, dirt, or non-slip pavement. Avoid steep hills and slippery surfaces.
**Reps:** Week 1 (5 mins), Week 2-3 (7-10 mins), Week 4+ (Adjust based on comfort)

### Exercise 3: Supported "Dancing" (Hip Extension & Balance)
**Purpose:** Activates hind end and improves balance.
- Stand in front of your dog, facing them. Gently lift their front paws a few inches off the ground.
- Keep your back straight and support their chest. Hold for 2–5 seconds, then gently lower.
**Reps:** Week 1 (3 reps), Week 2-3 (4-6 reps), Week 4+ (6-8 reps)

### Exercise 4: Step-Ups (Low Impact)
**Purpose:** Strengthens hind legs and improves coordination.
- Use a low, stable step. Place front paws on the step first, then encourage hind paws up.
- Support with hands or harness as needed. Step back down.
**Reps:** Week 1 (3-5 reps), Week 2-3 (5-8 reps), Week 4+ (8-10 reps)

### Exercise 5: Backward Steps
**Purpose:** Activates hind legs and core gently.
- Stand in front of your dog, facing them. Hold a treat near their nose.
- Gently encourage them to step backward a few steps. Keep movement slow and controlled.
**Reps:** Week 1 (3-5 steps), Week 2-3 (5-8 steps), Week 4+ (8-10 steps)

### Exercise 6: Balance Surface (Soft Mat or Cushion)
**Purpose:** Improves balance and strengthens hind legs with minimal impact.
- Place a soft mat or cushions on a non-slip floor. Guide your dog to stand on it.
- Support them with your hands or harness. Hold for 5–10 seconds, then step off.
**Reps:** Week 1 (3 holds), Week 2-3 (4-5 holds), Week 4+ (6-8 holds)

### Exercise 7: Gentle Massage / Range-of-Motion (if vet approves)
**Purpose:** Helps circulation and keeps joints moving gently.
- Gently stroke the leg from paw to hip, then back down.
- Very gently bend and straighten the hip and knee (if your dog allows).
*Stop if they tense up, pull away, or seem uncomfortable.*

## 🏠 Section 5: Home Setup Quick Checklist
*Make your home safer for your senior dog:*
- [ ] Add non-slip rugs or mats on tile/wood floors.
- [ ] Limit or block stairs if they’re unsafe.
- [ ] Use ramps for sofa/car if needed.
- [ ] Provide an orthopedic bed with easy access.
- [ ] Use a rear-support harness for walks and stairs.
- [ ] Raise food/water bowls slightly if that helps with neck/shoulder strain.

## 🏥 Section 7: Questions to Ask Your Vet
*Take this checklist to your vet and discuss:*
- [ ] Does this look more orthopedic or neurologic?
- [ ] Which joints/spinal segments are most affected?
- [ ] What pain control plan is safest for my dog’s age and labs?
- [ ] Should we do X-rays? When would we consider advanced imaging?
- [ ] Can we get a referral to a rehab/physical therapy clinic?
