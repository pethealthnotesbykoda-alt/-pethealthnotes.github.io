Did you know that by the time a dog actually limps or cries out, their joint pain is usually quite severe?

It’s not because they are trying to be tough. It’s hardwired survival instinct. In the wild, showing weakness is dangerous, so our dogs will mask their pain from us until they physically can’t anymore.

When my dog Don first started showing signs of arthritis, he didn’t limp. He just... stopped wanting to jump into the car. He hesitated before using the stairs. 

I thought he was just "slowing down" because of his age. I was wrong.

**If you have a dog over the age of 7, here is the 2-minute home check you should do tonight:**

1. **The "Cold Start" Test:** Watch them get up after a long nap. Do they rely heavily on their front legs to hoist themselves up?
2. **The "Floor Slip":** Do their back legs splay out slightly when turning on hardwood or tile?
3. **The "Hidden Flinch":** Gently stroke down their spine to their tailbone. Do their back muscles twitch, or do they suddenly lick their lips? (Lip licking is a major sign of quiet stress/pain in dogs).

If you’re seeing these signs, don't panic. But *do* start making changes.
