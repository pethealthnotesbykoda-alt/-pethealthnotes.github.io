# Senior Dog Mobility: The 48-Hour Home Adjustment Checklist

*By Vishal, Founder of PetHealthNotes*  
*This checklist is designed to help you instantly improve your senior dog's comfort and mobility at home while you prepare for their vet visit. None of this replaces veterinary care.*

---

## 🛑 Phase 1: Immediate Safety (Do This Today)

When a dog's hind legs weaken, their biggest enemy is slipping. Every slip micro-tears ligaments and destroys their confidence.

- [ ] **The "Slip-Stop" Floor Audit:** Identify every uncarpeted path your dog takes to get to their bed, their water bowl, and the door. 
- [ ] **Lay Down Yoga Mats/Runners:** Place cheap, non-slip rubber-backed runners or yoga mats on those specific paths. *Do not let them walk on bare hardwood if they are actively struggling.*
- [ ] **Trim the "Grinch Feet":** Check the fur between your dog's paw pads. If the fur is growing past the pads, they are essentially walking on socks. Carefully trim it flush with the pads so the rubber of their paws can grip the floor.
- [ ] **Check Nail Length:** You should not hear a loud "click clack" when they walk. Long nails alter the angle of their foot and cause severe joint pain. Clip them or grind them down.

---

## 🛏️ Phase 2: Comfort & Setup (Do This Tomorrow)

Their resting environment dictates how much pain they will wake up in.

- [ ] **Audit the Bed:** Press your fist firmly into your dog's bed. If you can feel the hard floor underneath, the bed is useless for a senior dog. Upgrade to true, dense memory foam.
- [ ] **Elevate the Bowls:** Stooping down to eat shifts a massive amount of weight onto their front shoulders, which alters their posture and hurts their back. Raise their food and water bowls to chest height.
- [ ] **Block the Stairs:** A fall down the stairs can be fatal for a dog with weak hind legs. Use a baby gate to block access unless you are actively supporting them with a harness.
- [ ] **Car Access:** Stop letting them jump out of the SUV. Buy a ramp, or lift them out yourself by supporting their chest and under their pelvis.

---

## 🩺 Phase 3: The "Vet-Ready" Data Collection

Your vet only sees your dog for 15 minutes. They need *you* to provide the data from the other 23 hours of the day.

- [ ] **Film the Walk:** Take a 10-second video of your dog walking away from you, and walking toward you on a non-slip surface. (Vets use this to spot asymmetrical limps).
- [ ] **Film the "Get Up":** Take a video of them trying to stand up after a long nap. 
- [ ] **Log the Timing:** Do they struggle most in the morning? Or do they start strong but drag their feet after a 10-minute walk? Note the exact timing.
- [ ] **Download the PetHealthNotes App:** Plug these symptoms into the free tracker app to generate a clean, 1-page PDF report to hand to your vet.
